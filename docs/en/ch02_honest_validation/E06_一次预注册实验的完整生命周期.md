# Lecture 6 · The Full Lifecycle of One Pre-Registered Experiment: Two Verdicts

> Chapter 2 · Honest Validation (Lecture 3, chapter finale)
> Learning objectives: follow two real hypotheses from project initiation to verdict, mastering the operational details of a pre-registered experiment and the boundaries between the three verdicts.
> Prerequisites: Lectures 4 and 5.

## 1. Why These Two Cases

Lecture 5's all-FAIL was the fate of "batch candidates"; this lecture follows **the full lifecycle of individual hypotheses** — a human proposes an idea, writes the pre-registration, walks through validation, receives the verdict, and executes the verdict's consequences. The two cases, one REJECTED and one INCONCLUSIVE, exactly cover the two hardest operations: "what to do after a rejection" and "what to do when evidence is insufficient". (The SUPPORTED case is left for Chapter 4, to avoid structurally arriving at a happy ending too early.)

## 2. Case One: Earnings Forecast Event Window — REJECTED

### 2.1 Hypothesis

Around A-share earnings forecast disclosures there is a tradable mispricing window: stocks whose forecasts beat expectations continue to drift for several days after the event (a forecast version of post-earnings announcement drift, PEAD). Data basis: the earnings forecast (forecast) + former-name-change (namechange) panels.

### 2.2 Pre-registration essentials

Elements locked before the verdict (excerpt): event window definition, control group construction, OOS window, spread threshold (direction + magnitude + significance), and the boundaries of the three verdicts. **Archived before touching any OOS data.**

### 2.3 Verdict

> **OOS spread −0.49%, direction reversed. REJECTED, one-shot verdict.**

The discovery period (IS segment) likewise showed no usable positive spread — this detail matters: it is not the classic overfitting shape of "effective in discovery, fails out of sample"; it **never existed from beginning to end**. The hypothesis itself was wrong, not unlucky data.

### 2.4 Executing the Verdict — Where the Institution Shows Its Mettle

After REJECTED landed, three things happened, each worth telling:

1. **Hypothesis family ban**: the event-window family of hypotheses entered the "no re-test" list — no applying for another OOS with a different window definition or control construction. The same OOS window is a consumable, and it was already spent on this question.
2. **Data asset retention**: the forecast + namechange panels were stored as reusable assets. **What was rejected is the hypothesis, not the data** — data has no side; other hypotheses can use it later.
3. **Incidental observations downgraded**: along the way a hint of "post-forecast reversal" was noticed, but since it was not pre-registered and is a variant within the hypothesis family, it was flagged as hypothesis-generating (generates hypotheses only, constitutes no evidence) and **forbidden from entering the next validation round directly**. To pursue it, write a new pre-registration first.

Item 3 is the most subtle and the most frequently violated: "while validating A I happened to notice B seems to work" — that "noticing" itself has already consumed one look at the data. The institutionally correct handling is not to pretend you didn't see it, but to downgrade it to a hypothesis and run the full process again. What you noticed in passing is not evidence; it is a lead.

## 3. Case Two: Index Rebalancing Flow — INCONCLUSIVE

### 3.1 Hypothesis

When an index periodically rebalances, passive funds buy and sell in bulk per the announced list, creating predictable short-term price pressure (the inflow/outflow flow between the rebalancing announcement date and the effective date). This is a phenomenon with literature precedent (index rebalancing effect), one prior tier above AI-generated overlay factors (Lecture 3's conclusion two applies here).

### 3.2 Process and Assembly Validation

This case adds a real engineering step: **assembly testing**. The rebalancing-flow strategy's input is an assembly pipeline of "announced list → passive AUM estimate → flow direction sign"; any misalignment (dates, direction, weights) silently produces garbage. The assembly pipeline first passed a PASS check (sampling the assembled output against the announcement text), and only then entered pre-registration. **Validate the data pipeline first, then the strategy hypothesis** — the order cannot be reversed.

### 3.3 Verdict

> Discovery period +0.95%; OOS **+1.00%, p = 0.062. INCONCLUSIVE — insufficient power, frozen.**

### 3.4 The Correct Handling of INCONCLUSIVE (Harder than REJECTED)

REJECTED is quick and clean; INCONCLUSIVE tortures you — a p-value 0.038 short, and human nature screams "try again". The rules:

- **Freeze; no re-test with changed conventions**: extending the window a year, switching the statistical test, dropping a month — every one of these is "adjusting rules after seeing results", exactly the researcher degrees of freedom Lecture 4 locked down
- **No declaring "essentially effective"**: the external vocabulary has exactly three words; INCONCLUSIVE means it may not appear in any report's "effective strategies" column
- **Only two ways out**: wait for new forward data to accumulate (time for power), or write a **new pre-registration** for a forward round (register the thresholds before the next real rebalancing occurs, and judge with data that has not yet happened)

The case's actual disposition: OOS frozen, with a subsequent re-pre-registration on the forward route (the 2026-12 rebalancing round is within forward monitoring coverage). **"Waiting" is also part of the verdict** — accepting that "this question cannot be answered now" is the most anti-human and most money-saving rule in multiple-testing discipline.

## 4. The Decision Tree of Three Verdicts (Chapter Wrap-Up)

Stacking all cases from Lecture 4 through this lecture into one adjudication cheat sheet:

```
预注册验证结果
├─ 过全部门槛（DSR/副判据/分年/净费/正交）
│    → SUPPORTED → 影子监控 30 天（第四章）→ 部署决策
├─ 方向反 / 分年结构违规 / 正交后无残差
│    → REJECTED → 假设族封禁，数据保留，衍生观察降级
└─ 方向对但功效不足（p 略高、窗口短）
     → INCONCLUSIVE → 冻结：等前向数据 or 新预注册前向轮
                          （禁止：改门槛、改口径、原窗重测）
```

Note there is no edge in the tree leading to "close enough, deploy a small position first". **Position size is not a substitute for validation** — probing an unadjudicated hypothesis with real money is running a non-reproducible experiment at maximum cost.

## 5. Chapter 2 Summary

- Independent validation = institution, not compute: pre-registration locks down degrees of freedom, DSR corrects the selection base, one-shot OOS verdicts
- The fate of batch candidates (Lecture 5): wiped out across the four-layer audit stack; the AI factory's qualified output is a candidate hypothesis stream
- The fate of single hypotheses (this lecture): REJECTED bans the family and keeps the data, INCONCLUSIVE freezes and waits for forward data — a verdict's value lies in its execution
- The only surviving case annualizes just 2.37%: real alpha is small and ugly; fake alpha is big and plausible

Chapter 2 demonstrated that "honest validation" can be executed as an institution. Chapter 3 turns to its foundation: **when the data itself is broken, even the strictest validation gets silently contaminated** — double adjustment, benchmark artifacts, misaligned date stamps: five real data mines.

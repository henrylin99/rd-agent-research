    Lecture 13 · The Course Concludes: The Complete Puzzle of Research Discipline Through One Campaign¶

    Chapter 5 · Distilling the Method (second lecture, the course finale) Learning objectives: assemble the twelve lectures' concepts into one complete battle map; distill a list of research discipline you can take with you.

    1. The course's narrative thread, revisited¶

    Thirteen stories were told, but there is only one thread: when AI drives the cost of "generating hypotheses" to nearly zero, the center of value of the research system shifts wholesale to what comes after hypothesis generation.

    第一章  工厂很能干        290 个因子一晚上，代码质量过硬，假设质量平平
第二章  但成绩单不可信    多重检验 + 风格 beta + 净费 + ——独立验证零存活
第三章  连地基都要审      双重复权 + 基准伪影 + 五坑——数据与量定义的审计
第四章  幸存者依然谦卑    +2.37%/yr，现金管理级，判决只是获得认真对待的资格
第五章  瓶颈回到选择      排除清单 + 正交四问 + 经济门槛——便宜的否决保护昂贵的投入

    The numerical version of the thread (worth memorizing):

    Best self-reported +26.5% → independent validation 0 survivors; the fake carry +18% (adjustment bug) → the real carry +2.37%; REJECTED −0.49% / INCONCLUSIVE +1.00% (p=0.062) / SUPPORTED +2.37%. Real alpha is small and ugly; fake alpha is big and plausible.

    No individual component is new — pre-registration has been the clinical-trials standard for decades, multiple-testing correction is common knowledge in academic statistics, and shadow mode is the canary release of software engineering. The novelty of this system lies only in the consistency of its seriousness: when hypothesis production is accelerated a hundredfold by AI, the enforcement intensity of these old institutions must scale in step, or they degrade from "line of defense" into "decoration".

    3. Ten rules of research discipline to take with you¶

    The whole course compressed into ten, each annotated with its source lecture:

    1. On leaderboards, read the number-of-evaluations column before the returns column — combos/versions cap the luck component (E2)
    2. Ask three questions of every result: what's the benchmark, were costs counted, how many times was it tested (E2)
    3. Excess returns don't distinguish new money from old money; orthogonalization and source decomposition do — "alpha" that is 92% style-explained is not alpha (E3/E5/E10)
    4. Pre-registration is finished before touching the data; a rule change = redoing the experiment (E4)
    5. OOS is a consumable: one-shot verdicts, hypothesis-family embargoes, variant bans — all point to the same principle (E4/E6/E10)
    6. Audit the most expected-looking numbers first — being wrong in a flattering way is costly (E7)
    7. Fixing data is the last resort: change usage by convention, isolate artifacts, fix the documentation when the cognition was wrong (E9)
    8. Whether a conclusion depends on differences or levels determines its immunity class to data defects (E8)
    9. Monitoring breaks too, and keeps outputting certainty when broken — meta-monitoring and manual spot checks are necessities (E11)
    10. Write "what not to research" as formally as "what to research" (E12)

    4. The campaign-matrix endgame and "the compounding of negative results"¶

    The panorama of the real campaigns as of this course's writing (simplified):

    Campaign Ending Legacy
    A-shares Zero excess under the honest convention, DSR all FAIL the excess_honest convention, the validation institution, a complete specimen of fake alpha
    Futures r1 136 trials, postloop all FAIL the pre-registered validation pipeline itself, three of the five traps
    Futures r2 In progress (matrix system) six-direction screening, the D-9 erratum, L2 → watch line
    crypto r1 Top spot +10.85% not robust, actually a baseline empirical proof that "leaderboard top ≠ real signal"
    crypto r2 Real signals = vol_20d/reversal_5d (textbook factors) the reasonable expectation for the loop: rediscovering the classics, not inventing novelty
    funding carry SUPPORTED +2.37%/yr, in shadow the sole survivor, the pre-registered monitoring template
    Index rebalancing INCONCLUSIVE, moved to a forward round "waiting" is also a verdict
    Earnings forecasts REJECTED the template for the verdict-execution three-piece set

    Note the composition of the "Legacy" column: almost no legacy is a positive-return strategy; nearly all are institutions, conventions, templates, and specimens. This is not consolation — it is the real asset structure of this kind of research organization. Strategies decay; discipline compounds. A losing campaign that produced "a validation institution + erratum capability + an exclusion list" is far richer than a campaign that made money on unaudited conventions without knowing why. The latter's problem: its profits cannot be attributed, hence cannot be replicated, and no one knows when they will vanish.

    The compounding of negative results is the idea this course most wants to leave behind: record every REJECTED as a recomputable page in the archive, and your research system grows; forget them, and you are just paying the same tuition over and over.

    5. Closing: the researcher's place in the AI era¶

    Back to the sentence at the end of Lecture 1 — the full version can now be given:

    What the AI factor factory replaces is the "hands". But "judgment" — is the data trustworthy, is the convention honest, do the results warrant the selection base, is this direction worth a window — is not only not replaced; its scarcity is amplified a hundredfold, because the supply of hypotheses is amplified a hundredfold.

    Everything in this course is, in essence, thirteen facets of one thing: in an era of hypothesis surplus, the researcher's core skill migrates from "having good ideas" to "cheaply killing bad ideas". The factory produces cheaply; you veto cheaply — both ends of the system are "cheap", and what is saved in the middle are the years no longer spent paying real money for noise.

    Closing reflection question (no next lecture to collect answers; answer it yourself): suppose tomorrow a stronger model appears and the factory's hypothesis quality rises to researcher level. Which lectures of this course will become obsolete, and which will not? Why? (One line of thought: what becomes obsolete is descriptive content about "the boundary of AI capability"; what doesn't is institutional content about "validation and selection" — the former depreciates with technology, the latter appreciates with the supply of hypotheses.)
